Rook Dispatch · Final report

Release 4.2 quietly switched four responders off.

A shorter wait to answer a ping, a track record that never forgives a miss, and a console that can't tell a handler why. Here is what happened, the evidence from six modules, the fix, and the habit that stops it recurring.

What happened

Two changes shipped on 12 August. One of them set off a chain.

4.2 weighted proximity up (0.45 to 0.60) and cut the ping wait from 90 seconds to 60. The wait acts at the moment of answering, so it changed behavior first. A missed ping then counts exactly like a turn-down, and a score never recovers by itself.

Feedback loop: fewer pings means fewer chances to earn points back, so the quiet ones stay quiet. Nothing in the console tells a handler why.

Nobody has recorded why the wait was cut. Not the wiki, the code, the changelog or Priya's handoff. That is a finding in its own right.

MODULE 4 · X-RAY VISION

The code explains it: misses cost more than takes earn.

A responder needs to say yes at least 60% of the time just to hold their score. Before 4.2 the typical rate was about 77%. After, about 64%, barely above the line.

ACCEPTANCE_CREDIT = 0.08   # taken
DECLINE_PENALTY   = 0.12   # turned down OR missed
# no decay, no reset: scores never ease back
break-even 60%
~64% after 4.2
~77% before
score drifts down

Source: 00-rook/code/dispatch-routing/config.py and history.py. Break-even = 0.12 ÷ (0.12 + 0.08) = 60%. Wen Li's 2019 TODO already asked whether this should ease back.

The prompt I wroteFind me the part of this code that takes points off somebody when they miss a ping or turn one down. Show it to me and explain it in plain English. Then find me every single thing in this code that puts points back on.
The only thing that puts points back is taking a callout (+0.08). Nothing else restores a score, so a quiet responder has no route back.

MODULE 6 · SIDEKICKS

The habit: a review skill that checks every brief the same way.

Two things in this story were never written down: why the wait was cut, and why a committed feature (Availability Confidence) dropped out. The skill checks for exactly those gaps before a brief goes further.

The prompt I wroteUpdate the skill to check for a timeline, and look for logical inconsistencies
Run on my own brief, it said "fix first, 2 of 6 pass": owner is a role not a name, the next step is stale, there are no dates, and the reset depends on a wait fix the brief puts out of scope. I'll fix those before this goes to Helen.

It runs again every Monday morning (scheduled task) and reports what changed. It reviews only; it doesn't edit the brief.

Why this stops it happening again

Each gap that let 4.2 slip maps to a check.

The wait was cut with no recorded reason→Check 4: problem explained, with evidence, before the fix
Afterward we couldn't tell if the wait worked: response times aren't logged→Check 2: baseline, target and time frame
A committed feature dropped out with no conversation→Check 3: scope at the end matches scope at the start
Two changes landed together, so their effects can't be separated→Check 1: a named owner; plus Check 3 scope and a coverage line for dependencies and risks
"Mostly seasonal" rested on one person's read→Check 6 and coverage: assumptions marked, no claim stated two ways
No dates, no rollback→Check 5: timeline, plus a rollout line in coverage

The skill doesn't prevent bugs by itself. It makes sure the questions get asked in the brief, before shipping, every time. The left column is what we found missing afterward. I haven't seen 4.2's original brief, so these are gaps in the record, not claims about how it was written.
